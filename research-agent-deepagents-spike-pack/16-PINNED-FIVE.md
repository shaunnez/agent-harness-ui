# Five pinned scenarios, three runs each

22 September 2026. $20.91 of plan usage for 15 runs, $25.41 including the earlier
channel drain set. Opus 5 on the subscription throughout.

Four scenarios that QV should answer, plus solar mounting, which the routing said QV
would never carry — included deliberately to see whether the recipe survives a case
that has to go outside.

**Agreement and width are different things and should not be collapsed.** Agreement is
how close three independent runs land. Width is how uncertain a single run says the
answer is. Solar has the *best* agreement of all six (1.05x / 1.02x) and by far the
widest band (2.07x) — three runs agreeing that an answer is uncertain is a good
result, not a bad one.

Against the unpinned baseline of 3.39x, every pinned scenario now agrees within
1.25x on the low end.

## The tightest: cable at 1.02x

206–215, 204–214, 208–224 per metre. All four QV rows cited by all three runs. This is
effectively deterministic — pin the cable size, the route and the containment and the
answer stops moving. Cable was one of the items I wrongly reported as "no rates at
all" two days ago.

## Where the remaining variance is

**Ceiling, high end 1.56x.** All three found the same grid at 41.90/m² and the same
tile at 58.00/m². Runs 1 and 3 landed 100–120 and 100–105. Run 2 reached 164 because
it picked a premium tile at 120–122/m² instead. My scope said "medium acoustic grade",
which is not a specification. Naming an NRC value or a product tier closes it.

**Retaining wall, low end 1.25x.** One run at 581, two at 727. The one that came in
low left out an item the other two included.

**Solar, one run of three refused to band.** It could not price the proprietary rail
and clamp hardware from public sources and said so rather than guessing. The two that
did band went to the web for it and landed within 5% of each other. All three priced
the supplementary purlins from QV identically. The refusal is defensible and the
review surface has to treat it as an answer, not a failure.

## What this costs

$4.18 per scenario for three runs. The top 30 gaps would be about $125 of plan usage;
all 155 about $650. Neither is the constraint — reviewer time still is.

## What this does not tell you

No QS has ruled on a single one of these six numbers. Three runs agreeing means the
scope is specified well enough to be reproducible. It does not mean the answer is
right, and a confidently reproduced wrong number is worse than a noisy one.

The scopes are mine, not a QS's. I chose 1.8m timber pole for the retaining wall,
25mm² XLPE for the cable, 30 kWp for the solar array. Those choices decide the answer,
and whether they are the right representative cases is exactly the question a QS
should be asked — now with the evidence that pinning them is what makes the output
usable at all.
